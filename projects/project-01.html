Overview

Pick a public company, browse its 10-K, 10-Q and 8-K filings from SEC EDGAR, chart its reported financials, and ask questions answered only from the filing text — every claim cited to the exact excerpt and SEC.gov link.

Retrieval runs locally. Only the answer itself calls out, to gpt-oss-120b on Groq's free tier.

The problem

A 10-K is hundreds of pages, and the sentence that matters is rarely where you'd guess. Keyword search can't tell which match answers your question, and chatbots answer confidently about filings they haven't read.

The bar: every number, date or quote in an answer traces back to its excerpt — and when the filings don't have the answer, the tool says so.

How it works

EDGAR JSON APIs ──▶ sec/edgar.py     tickers, filing history, XBRL facts (rate-limited)
                    sec/parse.py     HTML → text, split on Item headings, cached to disk
                          │
                          ▼
                    rag/store.py     LangChain splitter → local embeddings → Chroma
                          │
                          ▼
                    rag/pipeline.py  similarity search → gpt-oss on Groq (streamed) → cited answer
                          │
                          ▼
                    app.py           Streamlit UI: Filings · Financials · Ask the filings

The retrieval pipeline

Plain dense retrieval — the document's structure does most of the work.

Parsing HTML → text. Table rows flatten to one line, so figures stay beside their labels. Cached on disk.
Sections Split on Item headings, so every chunk — and citation — carries a label like "Item 1A — Risk Factors".
Chunking 1,400 characters, 200 overlap (LangChain RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter).
Embeddings all-MiniLM-L6-v2, run locally.
Store Chroma on disk; chunks tagged with ticker, form, date, section and URL. Re-indexing a filing is a no-op.
Retrieval Top 8 by similarity, filterable by ticker and form.
Synthesis gpt-oss-120b on Groq, streamed. Every figure must be cited; nothing beyond the excerpts.

Decisions worth defending

Cache forever. Filings never change once accepted, so each is fetched and parsed once.

Free to run. Local embeddings plus Groq's free tier — indexing costs nothing and works offline.

Periods come from the data. Quarterly and year-to-date XBRL figures share a period end; the chart separates them by the span each covers.

Fail honestly. No match, and it says so. No API key or quota left, and it still shows the matching excerpts.

Safe to leave public. SEC requests are rate-limited, and questions are capped per day and per session.

What I'd do next

Hybrid BM25 search with reranking for oblique questions, table-aware extraction, and comparing a filing against last year's.
